UAD Doctor

See the −38 coming.

A menu-bar app for Universal Audio Apollo and UAD-2 rigs. It shows your UAD DSP load as a dot and a number, warns you when the host or the DSP is heading for trouble, and when your DAW freezes on "a UAD device is not responding (code −38)", it brings it back with one click instead of a power-cycle. Works with any DAW.

How it works

3 steps · no setup

01 Install and look up

A dot and a number appear in your menu bar: the same total DSP figure the UAD Meter shows, read straight from UA's engine. No window, no Dock icon.

02 Watch the colour

Green is normal. Yellow means save: DSP over 75%, your DAW skipping audio cycles, a clock unlock, or a recent UA error. Red means DSP over 90%, the DAW not responding, or the device gone.

03 Recover Now

When Live locks up waiting on your interface, one click restarts UA's engine and, only if it is still stuck, CoreAudio. Your DAW comes back the way it does after a power-cycle, without the power-cycle.

Lives in your menu bar

The UAD Meter averages every DSP chip, so it can say "plenty free" while one chip is missing deadlines. UAD Doctor watches the symptoms too: host overloads and audio-engine stalls in your DAW, UA's own error reports, clock lock and Thunderbolt link drops. It turns yellow before the dialog appears.

Evidence, not guesswork

Every UA error and every DAW stall is written to an incident folder: the UA issue report with per-chip load and missed deadlines, the last minutes of the macOS audio log, Thunderbolt state, top processes and your DAW's own log. The History Report lines them up so you can see which chip and which plug-ins were there every time.

Runs onmacOS 13 Ventura or later · Apple silicon and Intel · Signed and notarized
Works withAny DAW, and any Apollo or UAD-2 device managed by UA's Console software (UA Mixer Engine). Thunderbolt, USB and PCIe.
DSP readoutTotal DSP, program and memory load, device online state and clock lock, read from UA's engine over one persistent local connection. Same figures as the UAD Meter.
Host watchFinds whichever app is running audio on the UA device, counts its skipped audio cycles from CoreAudio's own log, and every few seconds asks it a harmless question; no answer within 4 seconds means frozen. Any DAW. Ableton Live's own log is read as well. Needs macOS 14 to identify the host automatically; on macOS 13 it recognises the common DAWs by name.
Recover NowRestarts UA Mixer Engine, waits for your DAW to answer, then restarts CoreAudio only if needed (asks for your password). Does nothing if the DAW is already responding.
UpdatesCheck for Updates in the menu, and an automatic daily check that shows "Update available" when there is one. Downloads come from this page; nothing installs itself.
WatchdogBuilt in. A new UA error report or a frozen DAW gives you a notification and saves an incident bundle: the UA report with per-chip load, the last minutes of the macOS audio log, Thunderbolt state, top processes and the DAW's log. Turn on Start at Login so it is always there.
PrivacyNo account, no analytics. The only network request is a once-a-day fetch of a small version file from this site to offer updates; it carries no identifiers. Everything else stays on your Mac.
