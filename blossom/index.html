How it works

3 steps · no theory required

01 Hold a chord key

Your keyboard's bottom octave becomes twelve chord-quality pads — maj, min7, sus4, dim, and more. Hold the quality you want.

02 Play a note

Whatever you play becomes the root. Blossom builds the full chord — voiced, spread, strummed, exactly how you set it.

03 It plays your synth

The chord goes straight out as MIDI — hardware synth or DAW instrument. Or run the AU/VST3 plugin right in your session.

See every chord as you play

The display names whatever harmony is sounding — hold two roots and it reads the combination. Every note lights up on the on-screen keyboard as it plays.

Fig. 02 — Chord display named live
Fig. 03 — Chord keys 19 qualities · combinable

Twelve chord keys under your left hand

The bottom octave is tinted on screen so you always know where the pads are. Click any pad to reassign it — nineteen qualities to pick from — and held keys combine: maj7 + add9 = maj9.

Shape the performance

A synced arp cycles the held chord in rhythm — locked to your DAW's clock or free-running. Key mode makes every white key play its correct diatonic chord. Strum and humanize add guitar-style rakes and human timing, with spread, mono, and automatic voice leading on top.
